This year's theme

Life is short. Chop am well.

Choplaif/chop · life/— to enjoy yourself, fully and without apology.

Bole Festival is Rivers State's yearly feast of the dishes we grew up on — roadside bole wrapped in old newspaper, soup pots that simmer from morning, fresh catch straight from the creeks. Every December we bring the best cooks from across the South together in one place.

For 2026, the theme is Choplaif: a celebration of good food, loud music and the people you share them with. Buy a plate, find a table, make a new friend. That's the whole plan.

On the menu

Taste the whole South

Bole is the headline, but the stalls stretch from the Niger Delta creeks to the Cross River. Filter by where a dish comes from, then plan your route.

Bole & Fish

The main event

Fisherman Soup

Fresh catch, periwinkle, prawns and crab in a fragrant, peppery broth. Best with a wrap of starch or plantain.

Bring water

Kekefia

Grated unripe plantain cooked down into a thick pottage with smoked fish, palm oil and scent leaves.

Comfort food

Onunu

Boiled yam and ripe plantain pounded together with palm oil — soft, sweet and served with fish pepper soup.

Gentle start

Banga Soup

Palm fruit extract simmered with beletete leaves and fresh catfish. Rich, aromatic and made for starch.

Deep flavour

Owo & Starch

A smooth palm-oil and potash soup with dried fish, eaten with springy cassava starch. Pure Warri Sunday.

Silky

Ukodo

Yam and goat meat slow-cooked in a lemongrass-scented pepper soup. The cure for a long festival night.

Warming

Creek Smoked Fish

Fish smoked low and slow the way the riverine communities do it, sold whole or shredded into your soup.

Smoky

Afang Soup

Shredded afang leaves and waterleaf cooked with periwinkle, beef and stockfish. Heavy, green and glorious.

Edikang Ikong

Fluted pumpkin and waterleaf loaded with meat and seafood — the soup Calabar is proud to be known for.

Programme

Three days, no dull moment

Cooking contests, live highlife, cultural troupes and a kids' kitchen. Times may shift slightly — the grills don't wait.

Friday

Fire Up

The grills are lit, the first plantain hits the coals and the city clocks out early. Opening day sets the pace.

  1. 12:00

    Gates open & grill lighting

    Vendors light up together at the main roasting line.

  2. 14:00

    Bole Masters — Heat One

    Twenty roadside roasters compete for a place in Saturday's final.

  3. 16:00

    Kids' Kitchen

    Little hands learn to wrap moi moi and pound onunu, with supervision.

  4. 18:00

    Cultural troupe procession

    Drummers and dancers from across Rivers move through the grounds.

  5. 20:00

    Highlife Night

    A live band on the main stage until late. Dance off your second plate.

Saturday

Choplaif Proper

The biggest day of the year. Full market, the finals, and the headline concert under the lights.

  1. 11:00

    Full market opens

    All 120+ stalls serving. Arrive early for the shortest queues.

  2. 13:00

    Pepper Soup Challenge

    How hot can you go? Five bowls, rising heat, one champion.

  3. 15:00

    Chef demo: Kekefia from scratch

    A Kalabari home cook walks through her grandmother's method.

  4. 17:00

    Bole Masters — The Final

    Five finalists, one grill each, and a judging panel that has eaten a lot of bole.

  5. 19:30

    Headline concert

    The night's big performance on the main stage. Bring your voice.

Sunday

Sunday Cool

Family day. Long tables, slower music and a proper send-off before the coals go out.

  1. 12:00

    Family lunch tables

    Shared tables across the lawn — order from any stall, sit anywhere.

  2. 14:00

    Stories from the pot

    Elders and cooks share the history behind the South's favourite dishes.

  3. 16:00

    Awards: Best Bole & Best Stall

    Crowd votes and judges' picks, announced on the main stage.

  4. 18:00

    Lantern parade & closing

    One last lap of the grounds, then see you next December.
