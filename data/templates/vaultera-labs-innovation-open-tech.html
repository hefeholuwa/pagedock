why we exist

Big enterprise tech left the door closed. We built a new one.

Digital transformation market

SAP and Oracle serve the top 1%.

Large enterprise ERP platforms dominate digital transformation spending — with large organizations capturing over 57% in 2024. SMEs, despite forming the backbone of the global economy, are consistently underserved.

Global SME reality

70% of employment — yet 67% fight to survive.

SMEs are critical to GDP and employment worldwide, yet the majority struggle to adopt digital tools due to cost, complexity, and software that simply wasn't built for them.

World Economic Forum

The second wall

64% of SMEs can't extract value from their own data.

Even digitized SMEs face a second wall — they have data but lack the tools to make it useful, leaving decision-making fragmented and manual.

sustainable by design

SDGs aren't a badge. They're the build spec.

We call it S-web development — sustainable web development. Every architecture decision is measured against a specific UN Sustainable Development Goal before it ships, not mapped onto one after the fact.

SDG 3 · Good health

Offline-first, not online-only.

Molaris caches check-in and records locally so clinics in low-connectivity provinces keep serving patients when the signal drops — healthcare access shouldn't depend on internet uptime.

SDG 4 · Quality education

Built for the data plan students actually have.

Abakada and Gamsys ship as lightweight, low-bandwidth tools by default — so a limited mobile data plan doesn't price a student out of the software.

SDG 8 · Decent work

Open-source stacks, not license fees.

ERPNext, WordPress, and open frameworks replace proprietary enterprise licenses — so SMEs redirect that budget into local hiring instead of vendor contracts.

SDG 9 · Industry & infrastructure

Modular systems, not throwaway ones.

Every product is architected to extend, not replace — fewer re-platforming cycles, less infrastructural waste, and systems that scale with the SME instead of outgrowing it.

SDG 17 · Partnerships

Research published, tools kept open.

Case studies, methodology, and select tooling like Abakada stay public — so the next lab, university, or SME doesn't start from zero.

how we build

One standard for bidirectional analysis.

Depending on the problem, we either research first and build next — or ship first, gather real feedback, then validate through research. Both tracks lead to software that actually works.

Research First

01
Identify the gap.

Find an underserved industry and define the core problem through observation.

02
Conduct the research.

Peer-reviewed academic research before writing a single line of code.

03
Build the product.

Ship a validated, research-backed solution to the client.

04
Document the impact.

Publish findings and record the case study for future reference.

Product First

01
Identify the problem.

Spot an urgent real-world problem that needs a solution now.

02
Ship the product.

Build and deploy a working solution, gathering real usage data from clients.

03
Gather feedback.

Collect user data and real-world results to start the research phase.

04
Validate through research.

Conduct peer-reviewed research to formally validate the product's effectiveness.
